# Implemented UI — Araccoon Hub

Updated 3 October 2026. The Cine Daily design system (handoff bundle in the repo root) replaced the brutalist theme. Current source is authoritative; the attached brief's old component names and 14-day reporting assumptions do not describe this app.

## Identity and foundations

Cine Daily: a three-note palette of near-black `#0A0A0A`, white and crimson `#C8202F`, with a pink `#E2457E` seam only where the dark hero hands off to the catalog. Bebas Neue (all-caps display, very large) and Archivo (body; 11px uppercase eyebrows with .32em tracking) are self-hosted via @fontsource because the CSP allows fonts from self only. `src/index.css` holds the design-system primitives (`--cd-*`, spacing, radii, easing, durations) and the semantic theme tokens: white page / black page in dark mode, inverted fill for selected states (`sun`), crimson for live/positive emphasis (`mint`) and actions (`primary`), a pale red tint for warnings (`coral`). Pills and circles for controls, 22px panels with hairline edges, no hard shadows. Editorial and motion primitives (Reveal, TrackingReveal, ScrambleText, SectionLabel, Blurb, IndexBadge, Sticker, Viewfinder, FilmStrip) live in `src/components/cine.tsx`; entrances replay on re-entry and render their final state under reduced motion. The storefront has a fixed nav over the hero (viewfinder, red disc, mega tracking title, film strip that turns red on scroll, featured picks on discs). The owner hub uses a black rail in both themes. No emoji.

**60/30/10 colour split (11 November 2026).** Each theme uses its page colour for about 60% of the storefront, the opposite colour for about 30% and crimson for about 10%: dark mode is black / white / red, light mode is white / black / red. How it is achieved:
- *Stage tokens* (`--stage-bg`, `--stage-fg`, `--stage-muted`, `--stage-line` …): the hero, picks curtain and footer follow the theme (black in dark mode, white in light mode) instead of always being black.
- *Paper* (`.paper`): in dark mode, product and collection cards are white sheets with the light tokens re-applied.
- *Inverse* (`.inverse`): the story section is a band in the page's opposite colour (white in dark mode, black in light mode); the story card inside it uses `--secondary`, so it is the opposite again.
- *Crimson band*: the footer wordmark sits on a full-width `#C8202F` band ("Raccoon" in the page colour, "Hub" in its opposite), which carries most of the 10% alongside the hero disc and the red buttons.
Measured on 11 November 2026 at 1440 px from screenshots (share of black + white + red pixels, photos and mascot art excluded): dark 60 / 30 / 10, light 64 / 27 / 10. Keep new large surfaces inside these roles; adding another big black or white block shifts the split.

The theme switch is a crimson curtain (12 November 2026, replacing the 11 November ink bleed, whose torn noise blobs looked messy mid-switch; the owner picked it from six options): a crimson (`--cd-red-500`) panel carrying the RACCOON HUB wordmark (white RACCOON, black HUB, footer-band type) sweeps in from the left over 430ms, holds the full screen for 170ms while the theme flips behind it, then sweeps out to the right over 470ms; the wordmark drifts left to right and fades in and out with it. It is a fixed overlay animated with the Web Animations API (`src/lib/themeCurtain.ts`, `.theme-curtain` in `src/index.css`), so it does not use View Transitions and the page is never shown half in each theme. The theme swap always happens exactly once and the overlay is always removed, even if an animation is cancelled. The mobile menu's close control is a copy of the menu disc in the same place whose two lines fold into a cross (and back on close). Rapid toggles are guarded; rejected transitions clean up their classes. Reduced motion, a hidden tab and browsers without the Web Animations API switch immediately. MotionConfig respects the user's preference, CSS suppresses nonessential motion, and chart entrance animations are disabled. Runtime reduced-motion emulation was unavailable; see the verification limits.

**Hero entrance: night reveal (14 November 2026).** The home hero opens black in both themes with only the mascot's eyes lit. The mascot is already in place as a silhouette in the opening's own black, so it cannot be seen. A small crimson dot drops from above the viewport, carrying a soft crimson light that rims the silhouette's outline, passes behind the mascot's head to the disc's centre, rests, and grows into the disc as the light fades, which shows the silhouette's outline; the mascot then fills with colour while the eye layer fades; the black opening then lifts to the theme's own hero background as the title letters arrive, followed by HUB, the taglines and year, then the navigation, category row and film strip, and last the description, Browse button and New badge. About 4 seconds in all; every cue is in `src/lib/heroCues.ts`. The eyes are a second copy of the hero artwork masked to two ellipses (positions are percentages of the 1200 x 1280 artwork, so they track at every size) with a soft amber glow, `rgba(232, 146, 42, .55)`, taken from the mascot's own iris colour; this glow is the only use of amber. The dot and the disc are one element, so the dot cannot land off-centre. Keyboard focus shows the navigation, category row or Browse button immediately. Reduced motion shows the finished hero at once. If the hero artwork is replaced, re-measure the two eye positions in `.cd-hero__eyes img`.

**Box flaps into and out of Your lists (13 November 2026).** Picked by the owner from six previewed ideas (ribbon drop, stash drawer, box flaps, title card, finds fly home, button bloom); the ribbon drop was built first and swapped for the flaps the same day. Going to `/stash` through any in-app link (nav stash button, Stash link, phone menu, "See your lists"), two crimson (`--cd-red-500`) flaps close from the top and bottom like the raccoon's box (430ms) and meet in the middle (1px overlap, no visible seam). Each flap carries the whole wordmark centred on the screen and shows only its half, so YOUR STASH (footer-band type: white YOUR, black STASH) comes together across the seam as they close. They hold 230ms while the page changes behind them, then open again (470ms). Leaving `/stash` through an in-app link plays the same, reading THE FINDS on the way to the storefront and RACCOON HUB elsewhere. Browser Back/Forward start with the flaps shut (set directly, so the new page never shows first) and only open them. Links to `/#section` addresses reload the page and change without the flaps. Fixed overlay animated with the Web Animations API (`src/lib/stashFlaps.ts`, `.stash-flaps` in `src/index.css`), driven by `useStashFlaps` in `Navbar`, which catches link clicks before React Router; the page change is awaited (capped at 1.5s) and the stash page's code starts loading as the flaps close. Reduced motion, a hidden tab and browsers without the Web Animations API change page at once.

## Storefront

Header includes identity, Browse, Owner Hub and a named theme control, plus a keyboard skip link. Search lives beside catalog discovery rather than competing with header navigation. The hero promise is “Small finds. Big everyday upgrades.” Its curiosity board draws only explicitly featured records with nonempty image URLs, newest first with ID ties, maximum three. Narrow screens show one preview; none/one/two records adapt without filler cards. Every preview opens the existing quick-view dialog. The Browse CTA scrolls to and focuses the catalog heading, respecting reduced motion.

Categories wrap and expose pressed state. Active filter text and a single Clear filters action appear together; result count is announced politely. Sorting labels reflect the actual criterion, including owner-supplied review counts. Named Bento and Classic buttons preserve current filters and focus. Bento retains wider spans without dense visual reordering; narrow grids use one column. Images use contain, with stable unavailable-image fallbacks. Public rating displays were removed because they are owner-supplied, not live Amazon ratings. Real owner badges remain part of the supported product model.

**Product cards (12 November 2026).** Cards send visitors to Quick View first, not straight to Amazon (`ProductCard.tsx`, `.product-card*` in `src/index.css`). A card shows, top to bottom: the white image frame with the save disc in its top-right corner, the category eyebrow with the owner's optional badge chip (e.g. "Crowd favourite", solid crimson), the title (two lines), the spec line (two lines) and a full-width "Quick view" bar pinned to the bottom. The bar is black (`--foreground` on `--background`, so black on the white card in both themes) and turns crimson while the card is hovered or the bar has keyboard focus; the card's hairline edge also turns crimson on hover. Clicking anywhere on the card opens Quick View: the bar is the card's one real button, and its `::after` covers the whole card, so there is no clickable div, Enter and Space work natively, the accessible name is "Quick view: <title>", and the dialog returns focus to it on close. Keyboard focus draws the 2px crimson ring around the whole card (`:has()`, with the button's own ring as the fallback). The save disc sits above that layer (`z-index: 2`), stops its click from bubbling, and never opens Quick View. The bar must not scale on press (a transform would shrink the `::after` hit area mid-click). No number badges and no "Pick" sticker on cards: featured finds are still shown in the hero board and the picks carousel. The Amazon button lives only in Quick View, which keeps each page's tracking source (storefront `product_card`, collection `collection_card`, article `article_card`). Effect on the 60/30/10 split: the catalog lost one crimson bar per card, so crimson is now mostly the hover/focus state, saved discs and owner badges inside the catalog; re-measure if the split matters.

**Find of the day (12 November 2026).** Between the catalog heading and the collections sits `.catalog-extras` (not in the hero, whose entrance choreography stays untouched) with the raccoon's find of the day (`DailyFind` in `src/components/DailyFind.tsx`, `.daily-find`): a white `paper` panel with the image, a crimson eyebrow "The raccoon's find of the day · 12 Nov", the title, the spec line, a black "Take a look" pill (crimson on hover/focus) and "A new one every day.", plus the box mascot from 640px. The whole panel opens Quick View the same way as a product card (the pill's `::after` covers it; focus ring on the panel). "New since last visit" tags and the "Pick up where you left off" strip were tried the same day and removed at the owner's request (they did not work well on the site and did not look right).

**Your stash: named lists (12 November 2026).** Like a wishlist with collections: visitors keep several named lists ("Your stash" first, then e.g. "Gift ideas for Riya", "Desk setup"), and a find can be in several. The bookmark disc on a card (top-right of the photo) and the "Save to a list" pill in Quick View work alike: an unsaved find goes straight into the list used last (one tap, like a wishlist heart) and the "Save to a list" panel opens (`ListPicker`, `.list-picker`); a saved find just opens the panel. The panel is not a modal: a heading, a crimson "Saved to …" line after a quick save, one checkbox row per list (name and count, 44px rows), a "New list" field with Add (the new list gets the find), "See your lists" and a black Done pill. Escape (without closing Quick View), Done and a click outside close it; focus goes to the first checkbox when it opens and back to the button on Escape/Done. On a card it floats over the photo as a white sheet (the card is lifted with `z-index` so the next row cannot cover it; light-scheme checkboxes in dark mode); in Quick View it opens on its own row under the pill. The disc fills crimson when the find is in any list; its name says which ("Saved in “Your stash”. Change lists: …"). The pill reads "Save to a list", "In “name”" or "In n lists". The stash button in the nav (below), a "Stash" item in the desktop nav and "Your stash" in the phone menu lead to `/stash`: "Your lists" title and a line saying lists stay in this browser, a row of list buttons (pressed state, name and count) with "New list", then the selected list's name with Share list (crimson), Rename and Delete (confirmation focusing Cancel), and its finds as standard product cards with Quick View; an empty list shows the box mascot and "Browse the finds". Share list copies (or, on touch devices, shares) a link to `/stash/shared`, a page with the list's name, "Someone picked these finds and shared the list with you", "Save a copy to your lists" (crimson) and the finds as cards. Missing finds are counted in a note, never shown. Both pages start at the top and are `noindex`.


**Stash button in the nav (12 November 2026).** Left of the theme toggle, on every page with the site nav (storefront, journal, collections, stash pages), at all widths: a bookmark in the same 44px outline circle as the theme toggle (`StashNavButton` in `src/components/StashNavButton.tsx`, `.stash-nav*` in `src/index.css`), linking to `/stash` (`aria-current="page"` there). The number of different saved finds sits in a 20px crimson badge at its top-right with white bold digits and a 2px ring in the page colour (hidden at zero, "99+" above 99). Its name carries the count: "Your stash, 3 saved finds" ("no saved finds yet" at zero). It follows the nav's tone like the theme toggle, so it reads over the hero and the catalog in both themes. **On Your lists itself it becomes a home button** (13 November 2026, owner's request, so visitors can see how to get back): a house icon in the same circle, no badge, named and titled by where it goes. It goes Back to the page the visitor came from in this tab ("Back to the finds" for the storefront or a find, "Back to the journal", "Back to the collection", "Back to the shared list", else "Back to the previous page"), using history so Back is not doubled, or to the storefront ("Go to the home page") when the visitor opened `/stash` directly. Either way the box flaps play (THE FINDS on the way to the storefront). **Save animation**, a mix of two of the six previewed ideas (crimson pulse and bookmark flip), picked by the owner: whenever a find is added to any list in this tab (card disc, Quick View pill, a ticked list, a new list, "Save a copy"), the circle fills crimson, the bookmark turns over edge-on and lands filled in white with a slight overshoot, two thin crimson rings ripple out (140ms and 320ms in), and the new count rolls up into the badge; after about 700ms the crimson drains and the outline icon returns (1100ms in all). Removals, renames and changes from another tab do not play it. It is CSS keyframes only, replayed by remounting keyed spans, and lives entirely inside `prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference`; with reduced motion the count simply changes. In Quick View the nav stays visible above the dimmed backdrop, so the effect is seen there too. The "Save to a list" panel's status line ("Saved to “Your stash”") is now a live region that is always in the page and filled just after the panel opens, so screen readers announce the save. The "Stash" text link in the desktop nav and "Your stash" in the phone menu are unchanged. The "Your stash n" link beside Surprise me was removed the same day at the owner's request, so the nav button is the one count shown on the storefront.

Beside the sort control sits "Surprise me". Each card has a 44px bookmark disc at the image's top-right that fills crimson when the find is in any list; quick view has the matching "Save to a list" pill, and a surprise find shows the box mascot with a caption and "Another one". The no-matches panel and the 404 page show the box mascot. Published collections appear as a row of link cards (`.collection-card`: first find's thumbnail, name in display type, count) between the catalog heading and the search field; with none, nothing is rendered. A collection page reuses the journal page shell: back link, "Collection" label, large title, description, count and the standard product cards with quick view. Catalog loading, empty catalog, no matches and request failure are distinct. Failure includes Retry; a genuinely empty catalog does not offer Clear filters. Browsing has no analytics dependency. Quick view shows the owner's optional "Why we picked it" note (`.pick-note`: eyebrow in crimson, a 2px crimson rule, the note in 17px medium) between the category and the description; with no note nothing is rendered. Quick view has a "Share this find" pill under the outbound action: touch devices get the system share sheet, others copy the link and see a toast. Quick view layout (12 November 2026): from 640px, the image and every action (Amazon bar, its note, Save to stash, Share, Another one) form a left column that is sticky inside the dialog's scroll area, and the category, pick note and description sit in a right column behind a hairline divider; phones stack image, actions, then details. Browsing inside Quick View (12 November 2026): when the page passes the list it shows, the photo gets white 44px previous/next discs on its edges (crimson on hover) and an "n / total" count at its foot. The Left/Right arrow keys (ignored while typing or with a modifier key) and a sideways touch swipe of 48px or more on the photo (`touch-action: pan-y`) do the same, and it wraps at both ends; a polite live region reads "Find n of total: title". Quick view preserves filters/layout, has a stable image fallback, readable description and a cobalt outbound action. Existing outbound timeout, tab reservation and deduplication remain. **UI/UX polish (13 November 2026).** Checklist and workflow: `.claude/skills/ui-ux-polish/SKILL.md` (it defers to this file). Conventions added:
- *Hover only where it exists.* Custom `:hover` rules in `src/index.css` sit inside `@media (hover: hover)` (Tailwind `hover:` already does), so a tap never leaves a card bar crimson or a pill inverted; `:active` and `:focus-visible` states stay unconditional.
- *Fixed nav clearance.* `html { scroll-padding-top: var(--nav-height) }` keeps anchor jumps and keyboard focus below the fixed nav; section `scroll-margin`s only hold offsets beyond that (story `-48px`, catalog heading `1rem`).
- *Wayfinding.* Journal and Stash use `NavLink` (`aria-current="page"`, the red diamond; crimson in the phone menu). Section links (Featured, Picks, Story, Browse) and "Browse the finds" use `useSectionLink` / `SectionLink` from `SiteChrome.tsx`: a smooth scroll on the storefront, elsewhere an in-app move to `/#id` and a jump once the section exists.
- *Phones (below 640px): two compact cards per row* in every product grid (storefront, collection, article, stash, shared list): 8px card padding, 9px eyebrow, 20px title, spec line hidden (Quick View has it), owner badge on the photo's top-left, 36px save disc with a 44px tap area, 38px Quick view bar; the list picker spans both columns. *One sideways-scrolling row of category pills* with a 40px fade at the right edge and the chosen pill scrolled into view; the sort pill grows beside Surprise me. Desktop and tablet cards are unchanged. Both were picked by the owner from looping previews.
- *Feedback.* Toasts rise and fade (240ms in, 160ms out), pause while hovered or focused (at least 1.5s left afterwards), and can carry one action (deleting a stash list offers Undo for 8s). The catalog result count is announced 600ms after typing stops; the search is kept in `?q=` after 500ms. The sort pill gets the crimson focus ring through `:has(select:focus-visible)`. `theme-color` is white in light mode and `#0A0A0A` in dark.
- *Type.* `h1`, `h2` and unclamped `h3` use `text-wrap: balance`; paragraphs use `pretty`.
- *Dead ends.* The 404 page adds "Read the journal" and "Your stash" links and centres the red disc on the numeral with the heading below it; an article ends with "Back to the journal" and "Browse the finds". Quick View shows the owner badge beside the category.

## Owner workspace and forms

Desktop uses the existing navigation rail; mobile/tablet use wrapping navigation above the workspace with storefront return, theme and sign-out controls. Products has a "Check listings" action; a find whose Amazon listing returned "not found" shows a "Gone?" chip there and a banner on the Overview. Collections (list, plus an editor dialog with title, description, status, order, an ordered list of chosen finds with move up/down/remove, and a searchable list to add from) sits after Products in the navigation. Products and Analytics remain primary work areas, while the supported Overview, Activity and Account destinations are retained. NavLink identifies the current destination programmatically. Sign-in offers Email & password and Passkey tabs (added 9 November 2026), with recovery codes for both.

The editor groups product details, image, Amazon destination and display details, reuses the API/shared schemas, and focuses the first invalid field. Existing server-backed optional Associates tag/sub-ID values are editable and preserved when saving. Field errors are associated via aria-invalid/aria-describedby and a summary announces validation. Failed saves preserve entered values and unlock submission. A synchronous guard prevents duplicate saves; close/cancel/Escape are blocked while writing or uploading. Closing a changed draft opens a safe unsaved-changes confirmation; no persistence across reloads is promised.

The image chooser is keyboard-accessible and displays actual formats/8 MB guidance. Selected/uploading/failed/stored states differ. Only a successful response sets the stored reference and “Image uploaded. Save the product to apply it.” Selecting/replacing/typing a URL clears stale success feedback. XHR uses actual byte progress and a 60-second timeout; object URLs are revoked. Other product fields survive upload failure. Successful real storage was not browser-verified.

Product deletion names the exact product or selected count, focuses Cancel, prevents concurrent deletion, blocks dismissal while pending and preserves the dialog with an inline error on failure. No Undo was invented. Duplicate, bulk and account actions retain supported behavior with submission guards. Account rename failures retain their values; account confirmation errors are inside the modal. Failed sign-out does not falsely present a completed server logout.

## Reporting

Analytics is a refreshed snapshot, not “live.” Calendar period, timezone, generated time, retention metadata, visitor-day definition and recorded conversion meaning are explicit. Summary and conversion requests settle independently. Initial failures show unavailable values (dashes), not zero; refresh failures retain the previous report with a warning. Request sequencing prevents slow older periods overwriting newer selections.

Charts have units and an accessible daily-count table; animation is disabled. Summary panels stack on narrow screens and tables have contained horizontal scrolling. Recent activity and conversion records show their actual 50/200-record scopes. CSV remains the real server-backed rolling-period export, with its 50,000-row cap and difference from calendar dates disclosed. Spreadsheet protections were retained. Zero activity follows a successful response; it is never substituted for failed responses.
